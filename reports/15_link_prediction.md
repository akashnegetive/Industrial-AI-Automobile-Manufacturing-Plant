# Part E3 (bonus) – Suggesting missing links with node embeddings

Generated by `python -m ddn.linkpred`. Spectral node embeddings (truncated SVD of the normalised adjacency) over records, assets, text tokens, the hierarchy and every approved link; score = cosine similarity. Evaluated out-of-fold (5 folds over the labelled records; a fold's own links are hidden before fitting).

## How good is it on its own?

| Records | n | Link prediction hit@1 | hit@5 | Matcher top-1 (out-of-fold) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| tag | 539 | 0.954 | 0.996 | 1.000 |
| workorder | 888 | 0.739 | 0.946 | 0.982 |
| all | 1427 | 0.820 | 0.965 | 0.989 |

Link prediction is **weaker than the matcher overall**. That is expected: the matcher uses 20 designed features and a candidate list, while link prediction ranks all 455 assets from graph structure alone. Its value is as an independent **second opinion** where the matcher is unsure. (The two settings CODE_WEIGHT=3, ALPHA=1 were chosen from 6 combinations on these same folds, so the numbers are slightly optimistic.) A first version with a spectral (SVD) embedding reached only hit@1 0.03: siblings such as robot 07 on three lines share their tokens, and only the aggregated approved links separate them.

## Where it helps: records the matcher got wrong or was unsure about

- Labelled records the matcher was unsure about (below auto-accept) or got wrong: **20**. Link prediction hit@1 **0.30**, hit@3 **0.75**, hit@5 **0.85**.
- Of the 16 labelled records whose out-of-fold top candidate was wrong, link prediction had the right asset first for **3** and in its top 3 for **11**.
- So on hard records its top-1 alone is not trustworthy, but its top 3 usually contains the answer. That is why it is routed as a short list for an engineer, never auto-accepted.
